## Proposed service positioning

### TDG — Trusted Data Gateway Provider

Describe TDG as a practical consultancy and operating approach for helping a factory or supplier **organize, validate, govern, and share product and production data with the right buyer, brand, or DPP system**. The key idea is a trustworthy, permissioned route from data created in the supply chain to the people and systems that need it.

Show the TDG journey as:

1. **Find the source:** locate information in product specifications, bills of materials, purchase orders, supplier records, certificates, production systems, spreadsheets, and other approved sources.
2. **Agree the meaning:** map the fields, product references, suppliers, production locations, orders, batches/lots, and evidence to a clear data dictionary.
3. **Check and label the evidence:** record source, owner, date, review status, and supporting document. Distinguish supplier-provided data from reviewed or independently assured data; never call every field “verified” by default.
4. **Set sharing rules:** agree data ownership, purpose, permissions, confidentiality, access levels, update responsibilities, and correction history with the relevant parties.
5. **Connect and exchange:** use an appropriate existing workflow—such as a structured template, CSV export, portal, or system integration—to share approved data with a buyer or a client-selected DPP platform.
6. **Maintain and improve:** track gaps, stale records, failed handoffs, and supplier questions, then improve the flow over time.

Possible TDG deliverables include a current-state data-flow map, source-to-field map, data dictionary, evidence checklist, sharing/role matrix, integration options, supplier onboarding pack, and an agreed operating procedure. Confirm Deshik's exact implementation and technology capabilities before describing any connector, gateway product, hosting, independent verification, or managed service as currently available.

**Trust and data-ownership rule:** Present factory and supplier data as controlled by its owner and shared only under agreed permission. Do not imply that Deshik owns the source data, can disclose it to every buyer, or independently certifies every claim. Use “trusted” to describe transparent provenance and controlled handling, not an unearned accreditation.

### DPP — Digital Product Passport

Explain that a DPP is more than a QR code: the visible carrier is connected to product information, identifiers, data sources, update processes, and access rules. The website should show how Deshik's consultancy could help a client:

- Understand its market/product scope and its role in a value chain.
- Assess which product and supplier data already exist and where they live.
- Identify gaps in product identifiers, materials, production traceability, supporting evidence, systems, people, and governance.
- Build a staged plan for collecting and structuring data, engaging suppliers, and preparing a pilot.
- Test a sample passport or buyer-facing data view using a client-selected or partner platform, if that capability is confirmed.
- Prepare a scalable data and supplier process that can adapt as the textile delegated act and relevant standards evolve.

Potential data examples may include product/style identification, material composition, supplier and production-stage details, supporting documentation, and product-use or end-of-life information. Present these as **preparation topics or illustrative fields**, not as a final legally mandated textile checklist. The final fields, data granularity, carrier, and access rules depend on the applicable legal act.

### Best practices to adapt for Deshik

- **Start with a useful self-assessment, then offer depth:** a quick readiness check should lead to a clear, optional workshop or evidence-based gap assessment—not a forced software sale.
- **Give customers tangible outputs:** readiness baseline, data gap list, supplier map, source-to-field matrix, TDG process design, pilot, implementation roadmap, and role-specific training.
- **Pilot before scaling:** use one agreed product/style and purchase order or batch to expose data, supplier, system, and communication issues before a wider rollout.
- **Collect once and reuse responsibly:** map existing records first, reduce duplicate supplier requests, and reuse validated information only with agreed permissions and context.
- **Lead with evidence and provenance:** show where information came from, who owns it, when it was checked, and whether it is supplier-declared, document-reviewed, or independently assured.
- **Build supplier participation and value:** clear templates, accessible guidance, hands-on onboarding, named support, fair treatment of confidential data, and a feedback loop for suppliers.
- **Design for interoperability:** use appropriate open or recognized identifiers, data formats, and interfaces where they fit; preserve data export and avoid unnecessary lock-in. Do not prescribe a single vendor or imply every company needs blockchain.
- **Address both systems and people:** integrate with existing ERP/PLM/MES or buyer workflows when justified, but also assign business owners and train staff.
- **Keep public-facing data useful:** a simple product view for a customer can coexist with more detailed permissioned data for business partners. Never expose supplier-confidential or personal information in a public demo.
- **Explain limits and responsibilities:** consultancy, software provision, independent verification, certification, and legal advice are different services. State which role Deshik actually performs and which requires a partner.
- **Communicate business value carefully:** re-usable records, faster buyer responses, improved product-data visibility, and circularity opportunities are reasonable goals to explore, not guaranteed outcomes.

## Regulatory and factual accuracy rules

The website is informational, not legal advice. Use a dated, cautious explanation of the regulatory position and review it immediately before publication.

- The EU's Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (**ESPR**, Regulation (EU) 2024/1781) establishes the framework for product-specific rules and DPPs. The regulation requires DPP information, when applicable, to be accurate, complete, and up to date; a DPP is connected to a persistent unique product identifier via a data carrier; and the regulation calls for open, interoperable data without vendor lock-in. [EUR-Lex — Regulation (EU) 2024/1781](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1781/oj/eng)
- The Commission's **2025–2030 Working Plan** lists textiles/apparel as a priority and gives **2027 as the indicative adoption year** for product requirements. This is an indicative work-plan date, **not a fixed textile DPP compliance deadline**. Check for later Commission updates before publication. [European Commission — COM(2025) 187 final](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f7ff5e2-ebe9-4bd4-a139-db881bd6398f_en?filename=FAQ-UPDATE-4th-Iteration_clean.pdf)
- A Joint Research Centre study published in May 2026 proposes textile-apparel DPP content and discusses identifiers, product/producer information, compliance evidence, granularity, access rights, and governance. It is preparatory research, **not the final delegated act**. Do not present its proposed fields as binding law. [JRC — Study on DPP content for textile apparel products under ESPR](https://susproc.jrc.ec.europa.eu/product-bureau/sites/default/files/2026-05/Textiles_DPP_20260513.pdf)
- The exact textile product scope, required fields, identification granularity, data-carrier placement, access rights, and application date depend on the applicable legal acts. Do not promise that every garment needs the same QR, item-level identifier, or data fields today.
- The ESPR assigns responsibilities to the economic operator placing the product on the EU market. A Bangladesh factory may be an important source of product and production data, but do not claim that every upstream factory has the same direct legal responsibility. The exact role must be assessed for the particular product, customer, market, and final rules.
- Do not write “textile DPP is already mandatory from 2027” or “all Bangladesh factories must comply by [fixed date]” unless a current, authoritative legal source supports the specific statement.
- Do not describe a readiness score or pilot as proof of compliance. Do not claim EU approval, accreditation, certification, legal representation, or independent verification without documented authority and scope.
- GS1 standards may be relevant to identifiers and carriers, but do not say that every client must adopt a particular identifier or QR format before the product-specific requirements and client needs are assessed. [GS1 — Apparel standards](https://gs1.se/en/your-industry/retail/apparel/) · [GS1 — 2D barcode implementation guidance](https://www.gs1.org/standards/2d-barcodes-retail-point-sale-implementation-guideline/current-standard)
